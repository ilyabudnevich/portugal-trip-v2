# Usability Audit — Portugal Trip Dashboard

**Method.** Rendered and measured the live app at two viewports: desktop (1427×698 CSS px, 880px content column) and mobile at exactly 390×844 via a same-origin iframe — Chrome clamps window width above 390, so the iframe was the only way to get the real mobile media-query branch. All numbers below are measured from the rendered DOM, not read off the CSS. No files were changed.

Two measurement caveats, stated up front: dark mode contrast is computed from the design tokens rather than rendered, because `prefers-color-scheme` can't be emulated through these tools; and iOS sticky-hover is inferred from the CSS rule set plus the measured default tap-highlight, since this is desktop Chrome, not Safari on a phone.

One non-finding, so it isn't mistaken for one: the load fires **16** API requests, each table twice. That's `StrictMode` in `src/main.jsx:7` double-invoking the effect in dev. Production fires 8.

---

## 1. The during-trip job

**F1 — There is no concept of "today." — blocker**
Measured: `[class*=today], [data-today]` → **0 elements**. At 390px the first screen contains the header and Bookings only; **no itinerary content is above the fold**. The first day card starts at y=889 (1.05 screens down), and mid-trip days sit far below: Sep 2 at 4.1 screens, Sep 4 at 5.5, Sep 5 at 6.0, Sep 6 at 6.6. To answer "what's today" she scrolls ~4 screens and reads dates to find her place.

This is the primary job and the app does not serve it. Everything else in this report is secondary.

*Smallest smart fix:* today anchor — compute today's date, mark that card (`aria-current="date"` plus a TODAY label and accent), and `scrollIntoView` it once data lands. Before Aug 28 anchor to day 1; after Sep 6, to the last day. **Effort S. Behavior + minor CSS.**

**F2 — Bookings occupy the entire first screen. — major**
Measured: 661px tall (8.4% of the document), sitting above the itinerary. Flights and hotels are display-only reference needed twice in ten days, yet they are the first thing both users see on every open.

*Fix:* move Bookings below the Itinerary, or collapse to a one-line summary. **Effort S. Layout.**

**F3 — Scroll position is lost on every reload. — major**
Measured: scrolled to y=3489 (Sep 2), reloaded, `scrollY` came back **0** with no day card visible at all. Native scroll restoration can't work because the `Loading trip…` state collapses the document to a few hundred pixels before the real content arrives. During the trip she will reload constantly — flaky hotel wifi, returning to Safari from other apps.

*Fix:* subsumed by F1. Restoring *today* is better than restoring an arbitrary offset. **Effort S (same change). Behavior.**

**F4 — Loading is all-or-nothing. — major**
Measured: 8 GETs issued in parallel (max start spread <50ms), slowest 244ms, wall time 249ms on good wifi, all gated behind a single `Promise.all`. One slow table blanks the entire app, including the itinerary. No service worker (`serviceWorker.controller` → false), no cache — offline shows nothing.

*Fix:* stage the load so the itinerary paints as soon as its three queries resolve. Separately, "stale-while-revalidate from localStorage" — persist the last good fetch, render it instantly, revalidate behind it — is the genuinely valuable version for a trip. **Effort M. Behavior.**

**F5 — A failed load replaces the whole page with one bare sentence and no way to retry. — major**
On poor wifi this is the state she will actually meet. There is no retry, and no fallback to last-known data.

*Fix:* keep last-good data when available; otherwise show the message plus a **Try again** button. **Effort S. Behavior.**

**Glance vs. planning.** The 10-second glance is currently unserved: no today, no "what's next," no "what's undecided" summary, 9.4 screens of document. The 5-minute planning session is well served — every affordance he needs is present and direct. The asymmetry is the core problem, and it maps exactly onto the two users.

## 2. Mobile ergonomics at 390px

**F6 — Every interactive control fails the 44px guideline. — major**

| Control | Measured | vs. 44×44 | Failing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Status badge | 76.5 × **18.4** | 42% of min height | 63 of 63 |
| `×` delete | **16.3 × 15.0** = 245px² | **13%** of 1936px² | 83 of 83 |
| `+ Add` | 35 × **18.4** = 644px² | 33% | 16 of 16 |
| Packing/prep row | 325 × **40.4** | just under | 10 of 20 |
| Add input | — × **34.4** | under | 1 of 1 |

*Fix:* invisible hit-area expansion — padding on badge/`×`/`+ Add` with compensating negative margins, `min-height: 44px` on rows. Visual size unchanged. **Effort S–M. Mostly CSS-only.**

**F7 — Destructive and non-destructive controls are adjacent or overlapping. — major**
Measured: in itinerary rows the gap from badge right edge to `×` left edge is **4.2px**. In packing rows the gap is **−6.0px** — the `×` literally overlaps the item button's box (a consequence of `.packing-item { margin: 0 -6px }`), making the button's last 6px unreachable.

The asymmetry is what makes this serious: the **unguarded** control is the one that's easy to hit. Mis-tapping `×` opens a named confirm she can decline. Mis-tapping the badge **silently rewrites trip data** with no confirm, no undo, and no feedback beyond an 11px label changing.

**This interacts with F6 and they must ship together.** Expanding both hit areas while they sit 4.2px apart makes mis-taps *worse*, not better. The minimal correct version: push `×` to the row's right edge with ≥12px separation, then expand.

*Fix (minimal):* right-align `×` with real separation + expand. *Fix (better, later):* remove `×` from the default view behind a per-section **Edit** toggle. **Effort M. Layout + behavior.**

**F8 — iOS Safari will zoom the page every time she taps the add input. — major**
Measured: `.add-input` computed font-size **14px**; anything under 16px triggers auto-zoom on focus in iOS Safari, and it does not zoom back out. She'll be left at a zoomed viewport mid-list. Viewport meta is correct (`device-width`, zoom not blocked) — do **not** "fix" this by disabling user zoom.

*Fix:* `font-size: 16px`. **Effort S. CSS-only.** Highest ratio of annoyance-removed to characters-typed in this report.

**F9 — Sticky hover / tap highlight on iOS. — minor**
Measured: 4 `:hover` rules (`.packing-item:hover` changes background; badge/`×`/`+ Add` change color), and `-webkit-tap-highlight-color` is the browser default `rgba(0,0,0,0.18)` with no override anywhere. On iOS, tap applies `:hover` and it persists until the next tap elsewhere — a row she just tapped stays shaded, reading as "selected."

*Fix:* wrap hover rules in `@media (hover: hover)`; set an explicit tap-highlight. **Effort S. CSS-only.**

**Thumb reach.** Fine. `×` sits 37.6px from the right edge, comfortably in the thumb arc; rows span the full width. No action.

## 3. Density and scrolling on xs

Document is **7903px = 9.4 screens** at 390×844. Where it goes, and what's recoverable **without hiding information**:

| Consumer | Measured | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Option lists | **942px** (13 lists, 36 rows) | Always expanded |
| "Sunset ritual" rows | **614px** (8 identical rows) | Same sentence, 8× |
| Bookings section | 661px | F2 |
| `+ Add` rows in itinerary | 248px | 10 × ~25px |
| "Free day in…" rows | 163px | 6 rows saying nothing |
| Header | 143px | Fine |

**F10 — ~20% of the document is recoverable. — major**
Options (942px) + sunset ritual (614px) = **1556px**, about a fifth of total scroll.

*Fix (a):* collapse option lists to a tappable count — `Dinner · 6 options ▸`. The information stays one tap away, and this is *also* the right glance semantics: the glance wants "Dinner — undecided," not six restaurant names. **Effort M. Layout + behavior.**
*Fix (b):* the sunset ritual is a trip-wide ritual stored as 8 duplicate rows. Cheapest honest fix is to leave the data alone and collapse the presentation — render it once at the top of the itinerary, or as a small per-day glyph. **Effort M.**

## 4. Editing affordance discoverability

**F12 — Nothing signals that badges are tappable, and the visual language actively misleads. — major**
The status badge is styled by the same `.badge` rule as the **BOOKED** badges on flights and hotels, which are *not* interactive. Same shape, same size, same font — different behavior. The only interactivity cues are `cursor: pointer` (invisible on touch) and a hover border-color shift (unavailable on touch). Judged against her, with zero training: she has no way to learn that OPEN can be tapped, and no way to guess that BOOKED can't.

*Fix:* give interactive badges a persistent glyph the static ones lack — `OPEN ▸`, or a small chevron/dot. **Effort S. CSS-only plus a character of markup.**

**F13 — The `×` is effectively invisible. — minor (discoverability) / fails a11y**
Measured contrast **1.51:1** (`--line` #ddd0ac on #fffdf7), below the 3:1 non-text minimum. Quiet was the intent, and quiet is defensible for a destructive control — but 1.51:1 is past quiet into undiscoverable, while still being large enough to hit by accident. That combination is the worst of both. Folding into F7's edit-mode answer resolves it properly: absent by default, clearly visible at proper contrast when editing.

**F14 — Delete guarding is right; toggle guarding is the gap. — minor**
`window.confirm("Delete 'Ramiro'?")` names the item and renders as a real system sheet on iOS. Verified on all three lists: declining leaves every row intact. Keep it.

The toggle, by contrast, is unguarded, silent, has no undo, and is the largest and most mis-tappable target on the row. A confirm dialog on a two-state toggle would be worse than the problem — the fix is F6/F7 geometry, not a prompt.

**F23 — The add input has an ambiguous unsaved state. — minor**
Enter saves; Escape cancels; blur with empty text cancels; **blur with text does nothing** — the input stays open holding unsaved text with no indication and no Save affordance. On iOS she may well tap away expecting it to commit. This is current spec'd behavior, so changing it is a deliberate recommendation.

*Fix:* save on blur when non-empty, or add a small ✓. **Effort S. Behavior.**

## 5. Status communication, failure, and concurrency

**F16 — A hanging write is silently lost, and the UI claims success. — blocker**
Observed directly during slice-4 verification: PATCHes sat `pending` for minutes; the optimistic flip stayed on screen; **no error appeared**; the change never reached the database; a reload silently reverted it. Root cause: the revert-and-report path only fires on *rejection*, and `fetch` has no default timeout, so a stall is indistinguishable from success.

An app that says "packed" when nothing was saved is worse than one that says "failed," and she has no reason to distrust it. This is the most important finding in the report.

*Fix:* `AbortController` with an ~8s timeout on all five write functions. A stall becomes a rejection, which the existing revert + error path already handles correctly. **Effort S. Behavior.** Best value-per-effort here by a wide margin.

**F15 — No save-in-flight indication. — major**
Optimistic flip is instant and identical whether the write took 60ms or is still outstanding. On poor wifi there is no way to tell.

*Fix:* per-row pending state — subtle opacity or a small spinner until resolution. **Effort M. Behavior + CSS.**

**F17 — Error messages appear up to 5.6 screens from the action. — major**
Measured: the itinerary error line renders at y=847; the last day card is at y=5611 — **4764px apart**. Fail a save while editing Sep 5 and the explanation is six screens above; all she sees is a row quietly changing back. Also **0 `aria-live` regions**, so nothing is announced.

*Fix:* one fixed toast for save failures, with `aria-live="polite"`. **Effort M. Layout + behavior.**

**F18 — Two phones diverge silently and indefinitely. — major**
Measured: **zero network requests across 18 seconds of idle**. Data is fetched once at mount; no polling, no revalidation, no realtime channel. Concrete consequences for two concurrent editors:

- Last-write-wins per column — his toggle can be overwritten by her tap carrying stale state, invisibly to both.
- If he deletes a row still on her screen, her toggle hits the zero-row guard and she sees raw developer text: `packing_items — no row matched ilya/jeans`.
- She can add an item he just added; nothing dedupes.

Severity stops at major rather than blocker: two users, low-stakes data, and mostly non-overlapping editing in time.

*Fix (cheap, pre-trip):* refetch on `visibilitychange`/focus. This matches the real pattern — phone goes in pocket, comes back out. Plus humanize the zero-row message to "This item was removed on another device." **Effort S. Behavior.**
*Fix (right, later):* Supabase Realtime subscriptions — native scope or post-trip.

## 6. Heuristics and accessibility, briefly

**F19 — Contrast is marginal across the board, and fails in three places. — minor**

| Element | Measured | AA |
|---|---|---|
| Event text, badge-OPEN, `+ Add`, day date, option text | **4.45:1** | needs 4.5 — marginal fail |
| `badge-confirmed` (11px) | **3.21:1** | fail |
| `×`, `.day-index` | **1.51:1** | fail (3:1 non-text) |

A token tweak — darken `--ink-soft` slightly, `--gold` for the confirmed badge — clears all the text cases. Not rebranding; the palette identity is unaffected. Dark mode deserves the same pass. **Effort S. CSS-only.**

**F20 — Focus visibility is weak. — polish.** Only three selectors have `:focus-visible`, each shifting a color or border rather than showing a ring. `.packing-item` has none and relies on the UA default. *Fix:* one shared `:focus-visible { outline: 2px solid var(--azulejo); outline-offset: 2px }`. **S. CSS-only.**

**F21 — The add input has no accessible name. — polish.** Placeholder only. *Fix:* `aria-label`. **S.**

**F22 — One section is inconsistent. — polish.** `#trip-prep` is styled with inline styles duplicating what `#bookings`, `#itinerary-section`, and `#packing-list` get from a shared rule — the only section outside it, a leftover from the append-only CSS constraint in an earlier slice. *Fix:* add it to the shared selector, drop the inline style. **S. CSS-only.**

Roles are otherwise sound: `role="button"` + `tabIndex` + Enter/Space on badges, `aria-pressed` on checkboxes, `aria-label` on every `×`, sensible `h1`/`h2` structure, zoom not blocked.

## 7. State-model recommendation

**Recommendation: shared state everywhere. Build no per-user state — not before the trip, and not in the fall unless real use produces a need.**

The reasoning is one distinction: **every checkbox and badge in this app records a fact about the physical world, not an opinion.** Per-user state earns its complexity when the state is a personal view. Here it never is.

| Category | Recommendation | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Itinerary event status | **Shared** | "Is dinner decided?" is a household fact about a reservation. A table is booked or it isn't; two independent answers would be incoherent. Her confirming *answers* his question. |
| Prep checkmarks | **Shared** | Single-owner logistics tasks — "call JNcQUOI," "seat assignments." The question is "has anyone done this." Per-user would make doneness ambiguous. |
| Adds / deletes | **Shared** | These lists are shared documents. A private itinerary event is a different product. |
| Packing checkmarks | **Shared** | The interesting case — and it resolves the same way. |

**On packing specifically**, since that's where per-user intuition pulls hardest. The groups are *already* per-person — Ilya / Elina / Kids — so ownership is encoded in the data's shape, not needed in the checkbox. Realistically he packs his group, she packs hers and the kids'. Her ticking "Kids / Passport" answers a shared question; his ticking "Ilya / Jeans" is nobody else's business but also nobody else's contradiction. Two people ticking the same item independently would be meaningless: the jeans are in the bag or they aren't. Her checkmark **answers** his question; it does not pollute it.

**The need that looks like per-user state is actually filtering.** She wants "my stuff and the kids' stuff" without scrolling past his. That is a per-device UI preference — which groups are collapsed — belonging in `localStorage`, not a per-user data axis. Cheap, and it should be built with the collapsible-sections work in F10.

**What per-user would cost, spelled out, so the decision is informed rather than assumed:**

- **Identity.** The app has none — no auth, no user concept, no session. Real per-user state requires Supabase Auth. The shortcut (a locally chosen "who am I") is fake identity: it breaks the moment she opens the app on a second device, and it can't be trusted for merges.
- **Schema.** `checked` stops being a column and becomes `item_checks(group_id, item_id, user_id, checked)`. Every read becomes a join or a second query plus a client-side reduction; every write carries a user id; the composite key grows a third member.
- **Sync and merge.** Two axes of divergence instead of one. "Is it packed" becomes a reduction over users and you must *choose* the rule — any, or all — and then explain that choice in the UI.
- **Security theatre.** RLS is off today. Per-user rows in a two-person household immediately raise "can she see his checks?" — and the answer is yes, she should. So RLS would protect nothing and cost ceremony.

**Timing.** None of it. The state work worth doing before Aug 28 is making the *shared* model trustworthy: **F16** (timeout, so "saved" means saved) and **F18** (revalidate on focus, so two phones converge). Those two are what actually make shared state work for two concurrent users. Realtime sync is the natural fall/native item; per-user state is not on the roadmap at all until use demands it.

## 9. Proposed scope for one implementation prompt

**"Trust and glance" — all of A plus the cheapest of B.**

1. **Write timeout.** `AbortController`, ~8s, on all five write functions in `tripData.js`. Stalls become rejections the existing revert-and-report path already handles.
2. **Today anchor.** Mark today's day card (`aria-current="date"`, TODAY label, accent that reuses the existing city-accent pattern); scroll it into view once data lands. Before Aug 28 → day 1; after Sep 6 → last day.
3. **Revalidate on focus.** Refetch on `visibilitychange`/focus and replace state. Rewrite `no row matched` as "This item was removed on another device."
4. **Retry on load failure.** Keep the error line, add a Try again button.
5. **One save-failure toast**, fixed position, `aria-live="polite"`, replacing the three section-top error lines.
6. **Touch targets, done as one change:** right-align `×` at the row edge with ≥12px separation (fixing the −6px packing overlap), then expand badge, `×`, and `+ Add` hit areas to ≥44px via padding and compensating margins; `min-height: 44px` on rows. Visual sizes unchanged.
7. **iOS input hygiene.** `.add-input` to 16px; `aria-label`; save on blur when non-empty.
8. **CSS hygiene.** Shared `:focus-visible` ring; hover rules behind `@media (hover: hover)`; explicit `-webkit-tap-highlight-color`; darken `--ink-soft` and `--gold` to clear 4.5:1 in both light and dark; `×` to ≥3:1.
9. **Move Bookings below the Itinerary.**
10. **`#trip-prep` joins the shared section rule**, inline styles removed.

That is one coherent slice: it makes the app trustworthy (1, 3, 4, 5), answers "what's today" (2, 9), and makes it usable with a thumb (6, 7, 8).

**Explicitly recommending against, now:**

- Per-user state, user identity, or auth in any form
- Realtime subscriptions
- Service worker or offline caching
- The full edit-mode row redesign (F7-full) — unless it can land by ~Aug 22
- Swipe gestures, undo stacks, routing or multi-page, any component library
- Collapsible option lists and the sunset-ritual de-duplication — real wins, but M-effort each and they touch itinerary rendering that item 6 is already changing; sequence them after A is in real use
- Any visual rebranding — out of scope by instruction, and the palette is not the problem

**One process note.** Two of the A items — the write timeout and the concurrency behavior — are the hardest things to verify through browser automation in this environment, as slice 4 demonstrated. Both deserve a deliberate manual test on an actual iPhone with wifi toggled off mid-tap, and a two-phone test where you both edit the same row. Budget an evening for that rather than trusting an automated pass.
